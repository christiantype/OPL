The pitch

Gen-Skip Split-Dollar — an intergenerational split-dollar arrangement — preserves illiquid family assets (a business, real estate, private equity) for the next generation with unmatched tax-efficiency. A senior family member funds an ILIT to insure a junior life; at the junior's passing the death benefit lands in the trust, income-tax-free and outside the estate, funding the liquidity to keep the legacy intact.

Who it's for

Illiquid estate Wealth tied up in a business, real estate, or private equity that can't be sold to cover tax.
Low remaining exemption Little lifetime exemption left, so more of the estate is exposed to transfer tax.
Asset-rich, liquidity-poor A forced sale at death would mean selling the very thing the legacy is built on.
Wants to keep it in the family The goal is to pass the asset on intact — not liquidate it to pay the IRS.

How to position it

Lead with the problem the client already feels: "If something happened tomorrow, your family would have to sell the thing you spent a lifetime building, just to pay the tax."

Then show the gap — the two after-tax numbers, side by side — and let the difference do the talking. The goal isn't to dazzle with structure; it's for the client to understand exactly how their legacy stays intact.
