Wall

Every tile takes the shape of its own picture and the rows are justified, so the canvas fills edge to edge and nothing is ever cropped. More pictures means smaller tiles; the number of rows follows from their shapes. Tiles only swap to a picture of the same shape.

Whole photo fits the picture inside its tile so nothing is ever cropped; Fill the tile crops the overflow to leave no ground showing.

Stack

The pictures drop one after another and pile up, each landing a little askew — a stack of prints on a table.

Logo

Drop an SVG in the middle and the grid opens a hole for it, so the pictures sit around it. The hole is measured in tiles, so it always lands on the grid.

In Stack the logo sits behind the pile, running tall down the frame, and the prints land over it.

AW Mark

100 is as large as it fits. Past that it runs off the edges — set it high and the mark bleeds out of frame.

Duotone

Every picture is remapped by its own brightness — darks to the first colour, lights to the second — so a mixed set reads as one palette.

Each print that lands turns the pair further round the colour wheel, so the pile builds up in different colours. Zero keeps them all the same.

Texture

Both hide a low-resolution original: halftone rebuilds each picture as a field of dots, grain lays film noise over the whole frame.
